About

Milan Vojnovic is Professor of Data Science and Head of the Department of Statistics at the London School of Economics. His research is in machine learning, centred on how modern AI systems, including large language models, are trained, aligned, and evaluated, and how they can support trustworthy decision-making. Topics include reinforcement learning, efficient training at scale, multi-agent learning, and multicalibration, which ensures that a model’s predictions are reliable not only on average but across many overlapping subgroups. This work builds on earlier contributions to optimisation, distributed computing, and game theory.

Before joining LSE in 2016, he spent twelve years at Microsoft Research Cambridge, working on machine learning, distributed systems, and network optimisation. He was also a Visiting Scientist at Meta, where his work included multicalibration, active learning, and prediction problems on social network platforms. At Cambridge, he was an Affiliated Lecturer at the Statistical Laboratory, where he taught a course on contest theory.

He received his PhD in Technical Sciences from the École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL) in 2003, and his MSc and BSc in Electrical Engineering from the University of Split, Croatia. He is the author of Contest Theory: Incentive Mechanisms and Ranking Methods (Cambridge University Press, 2016).

His honours include the ACM SIGMETRICS Rising Star Researcher Award; the ERCIM Cor Baayen Award; best paper awards at ACM KDD (Applied Data Science Track), IEEE INFOCOM, ACM SIGMETRICS, and IEEE IWQoS; and research awards from Facebook, Criteo, and Huawei.
