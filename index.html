I am Professor of Data Science and Head of the Department of Statistics at the London School of Economics.

I develop algorithms and mathematical theory for reliable and efficient machine learning.

My recent research focuses on multicalibration, reinforcement learning, sequential decision-making, active learning, and scalable optimisation, with applications to large-scale AI systems.

I am interested in problems where learning is constrained by limited information, computation, or feedback, and where the behaviour of the system or its users can affect what can be learned.

Current research

Reliable machine learning

How can we make predictions reliable across many different populations?

Multicalibration provides a way to require predictive reliability not only on average, but across many overlapping subpopulations.

Recent work on MCGrad develops a scalable approach to multicalibration that automatically identifies populations where calibration can be improved. The method has been deployed across hundreds of production models at Meta.

A companion paper develops the mathematical theory of multicalibration gradient boosting, establishing convergence guarantees for the MCGrad procedure, including faster rates under additional smoothness assumptions. The paper has been accepted at NeurIPS 2026.

Reinforcement learning and alignment

What objective are modern reinforcement-learning algorithms actually optimising?

Recent work studies the mathematical foundations of reinforcement-learning methods used in the post-training of language models.

In What is the Alignment Objective of GRPO?, we analyse the objective optimised by Group Relative Policy Optimisation and clarify its relationship to its interpretation as an alignment method.

Learning with limited feedback

How should an algorithm learn when information is costly or incomplete?

Research on bandits, active learning, and sequential decision-making studies how an algorithm can decide what information to acquire while simultaneously making predictions or taking actions.

This includes work on combinatorial and infinitely-many-armed bandits, active learning under uncertainty, and algorithms with strong statistical guarantees.

Efficient learning and optimisation

How can machine learning scale when computation and communication are constrained?

Research in optimisation develops methods that exploit structure to reduce the computational and communication costs of learning, including distributed optimisation, stochastic optimisation, low-rank estimation, and communication-efficient learning.

Earlier work on QSGD introduced a method for reducing communication in distributed stochastic gradient descent while retaining convergence guarantees.

Theory and systems

A central part of my research is connecting mathematical theory with large-scale machine-learning systems.

My research has included industry work at Meta and Microsoft, spanning calibration, ranking, prediction, distributed learning, and optimisation.

I am particularly interested in problems where theoretical guarantees meet the constraints of real systems: limited feedback, computational budgets, communication costs, heterogeneous populations, and changing environments.

Earlier research

Earlier work includes distributed computing, consensus algorithms, information dissemination, ranking and inference, game theory, contests, and crowdsourcing.

These projects share a common theme with current research: understanding how to make decisions and learn from data when information is incomplete and other agents may influence the system.
